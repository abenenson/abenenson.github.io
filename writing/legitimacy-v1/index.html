Legitimacy v1.0.0

Machine-checked governance for the agentic layer of AI — six papers, read in order or any on its own. Each is mirrored as a signed PDF in the repository; for the framework itself, see the legitimacy project page.

paper 1July 2026
What the Legitimacy Compiler Found
The program in one pass: what a machine-checked governance compiler is, what it found when pointed at real agent harnesses, and why the governance layer is the unguarded surface.
paper 2July 2026
Semantic Legitimacy Kernels
The kernel object: a runtime layer plus an explicit bridge contract, inhabited by a concrete instance — a compiler target for the governance layer of AI agents.
paper 3July 2026
A Verified Impossibility Theorem for Peer-Relative Governance
No rule rationing scarce permission among an agent's competing requests can be consistent, solidary, and cross-claimant monotone at once — proved in Lean, and any legitimate rule must declare which it gives up.
paper 4July 2026
Which Governance Structures Survive Unbounded Capability?
Capacity and stability bounds: a governance graph stays stable at unbounded capability exactly when its consistency vulnerability is zero; any positive vulnerability gives a finite, computable stability cliff.
paper 5July 2026
Structural Audits of AI Safety Mechanisms
Source-walked audits of open agent-harness code, producing finite governance-graph fixtures with machine-checked diagnostic certificates over the extracted models.
paper 6July 2026
The Legitimacy Program in Context
Where the program sits relative to social choice, Guaranteed-Safe AI, and the verification trilemma.
